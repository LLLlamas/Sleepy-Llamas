Daytime Moonlog

The useful foundation already exists

The newer native iOS source includes multiple client families, twins, explicit shifts, core care logs, a caregiver’s note to parents, history, and plain-text and designed HTML handoffs. The existing “Day” appearance is a theme. Separate parent accounts, household invitations, report acknowledgment and versioned publication are new product work. Private CloudKit sync is deferred and would not by itself create those multiuser workflows.

No daytime/Talli strategy was found in the local research files searched. The native implementation is substantially ahead of the older PWA documentation, so future planning should use the native source and its status notes.

A specific competitive opportunity to test

Talli already supports caregiver sharing and broad baby tracking; Huckleberry, Baby Connect and Nara also cover much of the basic category. A prettier feed/diaper/nap tracker alone is a weak starting position. The proposed distinction is a reliable, personal handoff that parents can read quickly and respond to. Competitor marketing pages do not establish that this workflow is absent from every competing app; hands-on trials should test that hypothesis.

Talli currently states that its hardware manufacturing is paused and inventory sold out, while its apps remain available. That supports exploring phone-first simplicity; it does not establish unmet demand for another tracker. Talli shop notice; caregiver sharing.

Proposed report flow: Log the shift → review the draft → caregiver confirms the version → share → parent acknowledges receipt or asks a question.

Keep unresolved questions separate from report acknowledgment. Acknowledgment means receipt/review, not agreement with every care decision. Invoking the phone’s share action does not prove delivery or reading. For an early pilot, use an ordinary reply as manual acknowledgment; authenticated status requires a connected parent view.

What a parent should see

  1. At handoff: awake/asleep state, latest recorded feed and diaper, coverage window.
  2. During this shift: qualified feed totals, naps/sleep and diaper counts.
  3. A little about today: a few actual caregiver observations and what helped.
  4. For this evening: supplies, practical handoff information and unresolved questions.
  5. Your response: acknowledgment, note or proposed correction; expandable detailed timeline.

Use “amount not recorded” rather than zero when a feed amount is unknown, and “no diaper changes logged” rather than asserting none happened. Count a continuing nap only inside the coverage window. Show material corrections as a new report version. Start with deterministic totals and the caregiver’s own writing; any later AI editing should require review and preserve the original.

Scope in stages

First: day/night context, daytime note tags, completeness indicators, a review checklist, saved drafts, published snapshots and readable sharing from the existing native app. Address relevant recovery/export gaps. Next: secure parent viewer, separate identities, invitations, questions, acknowledgment and access removal. Later: concurrent offline logging and broader platforms.

Start with three to five consenting families already receiving care, then expand to a two-week pilot of roughly five caregivers and eight to ten families. Track report completion, review time, parent comprehension, follow-up texts and willingness to continue/pay. The agent’s rough development estimates assume an experienced developer and a clean current build; see the full product memo. Avoid committing to live family sync before the report itself proves useful.

Match the work to the family’s moment

  • Before birth: support people, care stations, household responsibilities and a first-days plan.
  • First day home: organize existing discharge instructions and appointments, practice everyday care and agree on tonight’s coverage.
  • Next days: three practical priorities, caregiver handoffs, rest coverage and questions to take to clinicians.
  • Second or subsequent baby: include the older child’s routines, different baby needs and co-parent roles.
  • Nanny or grandparent onboarding: demonstrate, practice, teach back, clarify permissions and write a consistent daily handoff.

For educational resources, use “What helps / What to avoid / When to call your care team.” Keep non-medical coaching distinct from clinical assessment. Nanny training should describe attendance or session completion truthfully, without implying an accredited credential or CPR instructor authorization.

Make the economics visible

At assumed total delivery times of 1.5 hours for a $125 call and five hours for a $350 visit including travel, gross revenue is approximately $83 and $70 per delivery hour respectively. Fees, tax, insurance, marketing, unpaid sales time and cancellations reduce that. Track actual preparation, writing and follow-up time before expanding packages. Overnight work must leave realistic recovery time for daytime appointments.

Get the first clients

Start with the relationship that makes a parent comfortable booking: real experience, a specific offer and an easy, working contact path. Concentrate home visits in a realistic Queens/nearby service zone and offer calls more broadly.

  1. Warm relationships: past families and existing professional contacts who already know Catalina’s work. Use personal, permission-aware invitations and referrals.
  2. Referral partners: birth/postpartum doulas, lactation professionals, pelvic-floor providers, local educators and nanny agencies. Explain scope and provide a one-page service sheet. These are prospective relationships, not existing endorsements.
  3. Local search: an accurate Google Business Profile if the business meets in-person service eligibility; appropriate service pages with real prices, location coverage and experience.
  4. Parent communities: participate where commercial posts, provider listings or workshops are allowed. Check current rules rather than assuming access.
  5. Paid tests: only after inquiry delivery and conversion are measurable; cap spending and judge cost per completed paid client, including delivery economics.

Google permits an eligible business that travels to customers to use a service-area profile; a home address should be hidden if customers are not served there. Online-only operations do not gain eligibility merely by selecting a service area. Google Business Profile guidelines.

Specific channel findings: DoulaMatch currently lists $25/year for most doulas’ first three years, with different listed rates for some groups and later years; check Catalina’s eligibility and rate. Raising Astoria limits self-promotion to direct requests, and its parent-business directory eligibility is unconfirmed. Gensa is the current business-facing destination associated with former Brooklyn Bridge Parents; its sponsorship prices were not publicly verified. Treat each as a channel to qualify, with permission and pricing checked before use. DoulaMatch pricing; Raising Astoria guidelines; Gensa for businesses.

Track source → qualified inquiry → booked → attended → paid → follow-up/referral. Do not count app downloads as consulting leads. A simple scenario is 25 qualified inquiries at 40% paid conversion to reach ten clients; that is an assumption to test, not a forecast. Include outreach time in acquisition cost.

A proposed first-month marketing cash cap is $250, excluding business setup and operating costs. Reserve paid promotion until the inquiry flow works and warm leads show willingness to buy. Do not presume an unquoted sponsorship can fit that budget. The detailed acquisition memo uses a more ambitious warm-lead funnel and a conservative downside case; actual counts should replace either scenario as soon as pilots begin.

A practical order of work

  1. Week 1: choose the initial offers and local zone; verify qualifications and capacity; fix inquiry delivery and contact details; prepare a one-page plan template and accurate booking terms.
  2. Weeks 2–4: launch focused calls and first-days-home visits; invite warm referrals and speak with prospective partners; complete paid pilots and record actual time.
  3. In parallel: test a reviewed daytime Moonlog handoff with a few consenting care families using existing sharing. Observe whether it removes uncertainty or adds work.
  4. Days 30–60: refine the two best offers, add bounded first-week support or caregiver sessions where demand supports them, publish genuine consented proof and useful resources.
  5. Days 60–90: repeat the channels producing paid clients at sustainable margins. Build connected parent acknowledgment only if the handoff pilot supports that investment.

The first concrete deliverable should be a bookable, credible offer and reliable inquiry flow. A complete standalone Talli competitor is a larger product decision that can be informed by real family use.

PRODUCT RECOMMENDATION Keep a shared care record and a shared codebase. Offer “Day care” and “Night care” as contexts, rather than splitting data into separate apps. Day care defaults: - Home says “Today.” - Naps and daytime activities. - Outings, play, comfort strategies and supplies. - End-of-day parent handoff. - Light appearance suggested. Night care defaults: - Home says “Tonight.” - Sleep stretches and overnight care. - Overnight settling and morning handoff. - Morning parent handoff. - Dark appearance suggested. These are editable preferences. Do not infer shift boundaries from sunrise, midnight or a timer. A caregiver may work an overnight-to-daytime shift or finish earlier than planned. For launch, focus on parents with an in-home nanny, postpartum doula or regular caregiver for babies under one year. Supporting preschool attendance, classrooms, staff payroll, every possible development milestone and medical management would dilute the initial use case. Possible positioning: “Moonlog, for every part of their day.” A separate daytime brand can be tested later; no trademark or naming clearance was performed. WHAT THE EOD EXPERIENCE SHOULD DO The report needs three layers: 1. A quick read: current state, latest feed/diaper, sleep total and important open questions. 2. A personal note: a few caregiver-written sentences about the day and what worked. 3. The details: a reliable timeline that parents can expand when needed. Avoid asking parents to interpret a dashboard before finding out whether the baby is still asleep or whether tomorrow’s supplies are running low. Recommended workflow: Logging → Review draft → Caregiver confirms → Published version → Parent acknowledges. Questions have their own status, rather than making the entire report “unapproved.” - Draft remains private to the caregiver preparing it. - “Review draft” checks the child, coverage window, missing details and still-running timers. - The caregiver explicitly confirms the report, then chooses whether to share. - “Shared” means the share action was invoked; it does not prove delivery, opening or reading. - A connected parent view can record “Opened” and an explicit “Acknowledged.” - Acknowledgment means receipt/review, not consent to every care decision, agreement with every recorded fact or a release of responsibility. - A parent can ask a question or propose a correction without silently rewriting another person’s record. - Changes after publication create a clearly labeled revision and reset acknowledgment for materially changed items. Use wording like “Reviewed by Catalina at 5:08 PM” and “Acknowledged by Ana at 5:24 PM.” Do not claim a report was reviewed by someone merely because they opened it. For the first pilot, text/HTML sharing can be paired with an ordinary reply acknowledging receipt. Label that as a manual process; do not present it as automated read receipts or authenticated in-app acknowledgment. Authenticated acknowledgment needs new connected identity, access and persistence services. ROLES AND OWNERSHIP Parent/household owner: Invite and remove members; see published reports; set care preferences; export family data. Caregiver: Log assigned-family care; review and publish their shift; correct records with attribution. Additional parent: Household-approved parent permissions. Temporary caregiver: Time-bounded access to explicitly selected children and necessary history. Avoid shared passwords. Do not expose all Catalina’s families when a parent is invited to one household. Set ownership clearly before implementation: a family should retain its records when a nanny leaves, while the caregiver’s authorship remains attributed. If caregivers need their own professional archive, define that policy explicitly instead of automatically copying a child’s full history into two owners’ accounts. MISSING DATA AND CORRECTIONS - Show “amount not recorded,” never convert an unknown feed amount into an apparent zero-volume feed. - Show “No diaper changes logged,” not “No diaper changes.” - Distinguish “not observed,” “not recorded,” “not applicable” and an explicit zero. - In a report covering 8 AM–5 PM, label totals “During this shift.” They are not full-day totals. - Keep nursing duration separate from measured bottle volume. - If a nap continues beyond departure, report “Asleep since 4:42 PM; still asleep at handoff.” Count only the portion inside the coverage window. - Preserve overlap/midnight/time-zone handling and avoid double-counting a nap across caregiver shifts. - Keep occurredAt, recordedAt, author, revision and source separately. - Resolve wrong-child entries visibly and provide an audit trail after publication. - A correction should update the facts and report version; a parent’s question belongs in a separate discussion record. There is a concrete existing-model gap: native totals currently add amountMl ?? 0. That is reasonable as an arithmetic implementation but insufficient as a parent-facing completeness model. The daytime product should also count measured, unmeasured and unknown-method feeds so its total is honestly qualified.
