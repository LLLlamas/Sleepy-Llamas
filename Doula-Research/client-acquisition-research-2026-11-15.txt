SLEEPY LLAMAS: HOW TO GET THE FIRST TEN CONSULTING CLIENTS
Research date: November 15, 2026
Prepared as strategy research. No messages were sent, listings created, ads purchased, or website code changed.

RECOMMENDATION

Start as a trusted local person with a small, clear paid offer, then make that offer easy for existing families and complementary professionals to refer. The best initial mix is warm family introductions, a small referral network, one permission-based community workshop, and free/low-cost high-intent listings. Treat social content as proof that helps referrals convert. Do not make Instagram follower growth or a consumer app launch the dependency for earning service revenue.

Use three buying situations rather than a generic promise of “parent advice”:
1. “We are bringing our first baby home and want someone to show us the practical routine.”
2. “We have a specific care/routine problem and need a calm plan we can actually follow.”
3. “Our nanny is starting and we want everyone caring for the baby consistently.”
For second-time parents, emphasize dividing attention, revisiting what worked before, sibling logistics, and coordinating caregivers. For the nanny offer, sell to the employing family first; family-specific onboarding is easier to explain than launching a new professional certification course.

CHANNELS, PRIORITY, AND VERIFIED COSTS

1) Existing and previous family relationships: highest initial trust.
Proposed action: ask a small number of appropriate past/current families for an introduction or permission to share a short new-service note. Give them a specific sentence and link to forward. They forward it themselves; do not request friends' private phone numbers. Prior employment does not automatically mean they endorse the new consulting offer. Cash cost can be $0; time is real. Target: four of the first ten clients. This target is a hypothesis, not research-proven demand.

2) Complementary local professionals: best repeatable referral hypothesis.
Priority partner types: birth doulas who do not provide this follow-up, childbirth educators, IBCLCs for nonclinical household support around their feeding plan, pelvic-health practices, prenatal yoga studios, and nanny placement agencies needing family-specific onboarding. Ask what their clients already request before pitching.

- Tiny Treasures is an NYC household staffing agency and also sells newborn-care training. It is both a potential partner and a competitor. A plausible proposal is family-specific onboarding following placement, not generic training that duplicates its course. https://www.tinytreasuresnyc.com/about and https://www.tinytreasuresnyc.com/pro-newborn-care-course

FIRST TEN CLIENTS: A HYPOTHESIS TO TEST OVER 60–90 DAYS

A qualified lead means someone with an in-scope need, a location/time that can be served, and willingness to consider the stated fee. Count distinct households once; a repeat call is repeat revenue, not a new client.

Illustrative funnel, deliberately visible assumptions:
- 20 individual warm introduction requests -> 8 opted-in qualified introductions -> 4 paid households.
- 12 carefully selected partner contacts -> 6 useful conversations -> 4 qualified introductions -> 2 paid households.
- One approved workshop: 18 registrations -> 10 attendees -> 4 qualified inquiries -> 2 paid households.
- Search/profile/directories combined -> 5 qualified inquiries -> 2 paid households.
Total: 21 qualified leads and 10 paid households, approximately 48% lead-to-paid conversion. This is an ambitious warm-lead test plan, not an established benchmark. Search may contribute zero initially. If conversion is 25%, 21 leads produce about five clients and reaching ten requires roughly 40 qualified leads. No claim is made that the named communities will supply those leads.

The workshop should solve a narrow task: “A calmer first evening home: setting up the feeding/diaper station, dividing parent rest, and handing care to another adult.” Keep scope practical, defer medical specifics to the family's clinical team, and leave time for questions. Offer one useful takeaway. Follow-up booking is optional; do not make access to the takeaway conditional on buying.

30 / 60 / 90 DAYS

Days 1–30: prove trust and delivery.
- Confirm actual work availability and three in-scope offers with fixed deliverables.
- Complete inquiry-delivery checks and the real contact details.
- Create a one-page provider introduction, an example care plan, and a fictional/anonymized handoff clearly marked as a sample.
- Have 8–10 warm conversations and 4–6 partner conversations; keep notes on exact language clients use.
- Establish eligible Google/DoulaMatch profiles, with owner approval of claims and personal details.
- Aim for 3 paid pilot households and feedback. A limited pilot price may be offered without any review obligation; avoid free extensive consultations.

Days 31–60: repeat the offer clients understood.
- Complete one approved partner/community workshop.
- Publish 3 useful pages answering the recurring buying situations, including English/Spanish support only if delivery capacity exists in both.
- Request honest feedback from all completed clients using the same neutral process.
- Aim for 6 cumulative paid households; record delivery time, follow-up load, and contribution per package.

Days 61–90: choose one scalable source.
- Aim for 10 cumulative paid households, not ten simultaneous clients.
- Choose the best two sources based on paid bookings and owner time.
- If warm leads convert and the diary has room, run one paid placement OR a small search test, not both simultaneously.
- Raise or restructure any package that creates too much unpaid follow-up/travel.
- Reserve recurring weekly support windows; pause promotion when capacity is full.

TINY BUDGET AND CAPACITY ASSUMPTIONS

Proposed first-month marketing cash cap: $250, excluding site work, insurance, operating software, taxes, and professional training. Example allocation: DoulaMatch $25 if applicable; simple printed leave-behind cards $30 (estimate); workshop materials/transport $45 (estimate); $50 contingency; up to $100 reserved for a later approved test only if inquiry tracking works. Spend can be below the cap. Gensa, PSP, or Mommybites sponsorship rates are unverified and not covered by an assumed $100 buy.

Founder capacity assumption while maintaining other work: approximately six service-delivery hours plus four sales/admin hours per week. Confirm this with Catalina. Start with limited bookable windows. Do not advertise continuous availability, unlimited messaging, or immediate emergency advice.

Illustrative economics using the proposed launch menu; these are example assumptions, not a forecast:
- 60-minute call at $125, with prep, notes and follow-up bringing total delivery time to 1.5 hours. Assume $5 variable cost. $120 contribution before owner labor, fixed overhead, tax, and acquisition.
- Three-hour home visit at $350, with travel, prep and follow-up bringing total delivery time to five hours. Assume $25 variable travel/payment/material cost. $325 contribution before owner labor, overhead, tax, and acquisition.
- Six calls + four visits = $2,150 revenue; 29 delivery hours; $130 variable costs.
- If acquisition consumes $200 cash plus 16 owner hours valued at $35/hour, full acquisition cost is $760, or $76 per acquired household. Cash CAC alone is $20 and hides substantial labor.
- Valuing all 45 acquisition+delivery hours at $35/hour gives $1,575 of owner labor. After the $130 variable costs, $200 cash acquisition costs and $1,575 labor valuation, $245 remains before fixed overhead and tax. This illustrates why bounded follow-up, travel limits, referrals and repeat work matter. It is not a profit forecast.
For paid advertising, set the allowable CAC from first-purchase contribution after owner delivery time, not from optimistic lifetime value. Until repeat purchases are measured, do not use them to justify an unprofitable first booking.

MEASUREMENT WITHOUT A COMPLEX CRM

One simple register can record lead ID, date, referral source, specific referring partner (with permission), requested offer, qualified yes/no and reason, fit call booked/attended, paid date, collected revenue, delivery hours, follow-up minutes, and outcome/next step. Track cancellations and refunds. Keep sensitive baby/family care details out of the marketing register.

Use a source field (“How did you hear about Catalina?”) plus source-specific links. A successful click or front-end success screen is not a received lead. Count lead-received only after actual durable receipt; count client-paid from the confirmed payment record. Weekly review: qualified leads, response time within published availability, lead-to-booked, attended-to-paid, cash CAC, full CAC including time, contribution per owner hour, and referral/repeat share. With a handful of leads, report counts as well as percentages.

Practical decision rules: if 10 qualified conversations yield zero paid bookings, revisit need/offer/price before more traffic; if four home visits consume far more hours than estimated, fix geography and scope; if a paid test reaches its $100 cap without a qualified lead, stop and inspect intent/landing page before adding money. These are proposed management rules, not statistical confidence thresholds.

TRUST, REVIEWS, AND PERMISSION

Ask for an honest review after service from all clients, not just happy ones. Do not trade a discount, free visit, or referral reward for a Google review. Google prohibits incentives and selective solicitation of positive reviews. https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114
Separately request written permission for any website quote, photo, case example, or name attribution; offer initials or general context. Never publish a baby's name, care log, medical history, identifiable home image, or nanny performance discussion as marketing material without the appropriate permission. Founder-written care descriptions can stand on their own until real reviews exist.

HOW MOONLOG FITS

Use a sample EOD handoff as tangible evidence of organized care. A family receiving a visit can opt to try the app and give product feedback; app participation should not be required for a useful consultation. App users can separately choose to learn about support, with an explicit local-service eligibility check. Do not assume downloads are qualified consulting leads, use care records for targeting, or market a parent-approved summary as a clinical assessment. The service business can supply useful workflow observations; the app remains a separate product with separate adoption and pricing validation.
