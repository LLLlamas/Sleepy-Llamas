SLEEPY LLAMAS: CONSULTING SERVICES AND WEBSITE RESEARCH
Research date: November 15, 2026
Prepared from local source files and current public primary sources. No website changes or outreach were made. Public prices are observed offers, not proof of demand, equivalent qualifications or provider availability. All Sleepy Llamas prices below are proposals to test, not changes to existing prices.

RECOMMENDATION
Position Sleepy Llamas as Catalina's practical newborn and family support: paid consultations, first-days-home visits, caregiver training, and overnight care. The strongest promise is: “We'll work through what's happening, practice what helps, and leave you with a clear plan.” Avoid an unlimited parenting advice hotline or promises to solve medical/developmental problems.

Launch three primary offers: Focused Parent Call, First Days Home Visit, and Caregiver Confidence Session. Offer one bounded First Week Together follow-up bundle. Retain overnight care as a separate established service. Test demand before building a large catalogue or subscription.

PROPOSED LAUNCH MENU — PRICING HYPOTHESES
Free 15-minute fit call: assess service fit, scope, timing and location. Label “fit call” or “meet Catalina,” not “free consultation.” Do not promise individualized care planning here.

A. Focused Parent Call — $125
Audience: one practical topic such as evening routine, caregiver handoff, setting up the day, visitors or preparing for a transition.
Includes: intake, 60-minute phone/video appointment, one-page action plan within one business day, one clarification email within seven days. Both parents/co-parents welcome without extra fee. No guaranteed “solution” or clinical assessment.

B. First Days Home Visit — $350
Audience: families wanting hands-on practice in their space.
Includes: three-hour home visit, three agreed priorities, practical setup/practice, written family routine/handoff, 20-minute follow-up within seven days. Available within a defined local zone, with travel quoted before booking. Not childcare plus an unlimited household task list.

C. Caregiver Confidence Session — $425
Audience: parent plus nanny, grandparent or other regular caregiver.
Includes: three-hour in-home workshop for up to two caregivers, family care instructions, skill practice, communication/handoff template, 20-minute check-in within seven days. Define family participation and privacy clearly.

D. First Week Together — $325
Audience: expecting/new parents needing bounded continuity, including subsequent babies.
Includes: 60-minute planning appointment, two 20-minute follow-ups over 14 days, shared family plan, up to two email question rounds. This is one bounded bundle, not a membership or hotline. Can be used instead of a standalone focused call; avoid charging twice for the same initial session.

SUPPORT CURRICULUM BY FAMILY MOMENT
Before arrival: identify support people; organize essential supplies and feeding/diaper stations; discuss visitors, meals, tasks and rest coverage. Deliver “our first days” plan, roles and contact sheet.

First day home: organize discharge instructions and appointments; locate supplies; practice everyday care within scope; set up safe infant sleep space; agree next caregiver handoff. Deliver a short “today/tonight” checklist. Do not interpret discharge instructions as a clinical provider.

Days 2–7: identify what is difficult; adjust household workflow; practice soothing/handling; organize questions for pediatric/obstetric team. Deliver three practical priorities and next check-in.

Second/subsequent baby: coordinate older-child routines, one-on-one time, coverage and co-parent responsibilities; revisit differences from previous experience. Deliver household plan including older child.

Nanny/grandparent begins: align family preferences, written care instructions, safety, outing permissions and communication; practice a clear handoff. Deliver family care guide and sample end-of-day report.

Return to work/new routine: agree caregiver responsibilities, workday communication and end-of-day transition. Deliver repeatable care/handoff routine.

For “do's and do nots,” use “What helps / What to avoid / When to call your care team,” avoiding a shaming rulebook. Teach through demonstration and teach-back. Attendance/completion records are not accredited professional certification. Being CPR certified does not authorize Catalina to certify other people in CPR; refer to a qualified instructor for that training.

BENEFITS CLAIMS NEED REWRITE
PaymentInfo currently broadly suggests HSA/FSA/Carrot reimbursement for doula and sleep training. FSAFEDS explicitly distinguishes emotional support, parenting information, childcare and housekeeping (listed as not eligible) from its narrow itemized medical-care category with additional documentation. That federal program is not every plan, but contradicts blanket eligibility promises.
Use: “Some employer benefits may reimburse eligible services. Confirm your specific service and provider requirements with your plan before booking. I can provide an accurate itemized receipt; reimbursement is not guaranteed.”
Never reclassify coaching or childcare as clinical care to obtain reimbursement. Do not promise immediate Carrot credentialing or a member's eligibility.
Primary sources:
https://www.fsafeds.gov/explore/hcfsa/expenses?q=doula&take=100
https://web.get-carrot.com/terms-conditions

SCOPE AND CLINICAL ESCALATION
DONA describes postpartum doula work as nonmedical support. Use this framework for service agreement/referral network; actual certifier may impose its own rules.
https://dona.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/DONA-Postpartum-Standards-of-Practice-2025.pdf

Practical curriculum includes safe sleep and knowing when to contact professionals, while avoiding diagnosis, medication dosing, wound assessment, feeding treatment plans or promises to resolve medical issues. NIH guidance supports back sleeping, firm/flat/level infant sleep surface and separate infant sleep space.
https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/safe-sleep/environment

Distinguish:
1. Practical coaching: household workflow, daily care education, communication, rest coverage, implementing existing instructions.
2. Clinical referral: symptoms, feeding pain or concerning intake/output, recovery complications, mental-health concerns, individualized medical/developmental assessment.
3. Urgent concern: stop routine consultation flow and direct family to immediate appropriate care; do not wait for a package follow-up.

AAP parent guidance treats fever in babies three months or younger as immediate pediatrician contact. CDC identifies urgent maternal warning signs during pregnancy and the year afterward. Link to maintained guidance; do not publish home-treatment protocols.
https://www.healthychildren.org/english/health-issues/conditions/fever/pages/fever-and-your-baby.aspx
https://www.cdc.gov/hearher/maternal-warning-signs/index.html

Maintain a resource path for families outside budget/scope. NYC provides no-cost newborn home visiting for eligible families with education and referral support. Complementary referral option, emphasizing why paid service must deliver personal convenience, continuity and application.
https://www.nyc.gov/site/doh/health/health-topics/pregnancy-newborn-visiting.page
