Explicitly include returning parents managing a baby and older children. The page should explain what happens in the paid session, what the written plan contains, the included follow-up, and when a home visit or clinical provider is the better fit.

SERVICE-PAGE COMPARISONS

1. Jane Frost — Postpartum Services (NYC)
https://www.janefrostdoula.com/postpartum-services
Current page; no reliable publication date displayed. It separates a three-hour postpartum visit from a two-hour virtual prenatal planning session. The planning offer includes a tailored printable plan and discusses helpers, rest, older children and boundaries. The visit includes a complimentary consultation plus remote support, education and household support.
Buyer expectation: a planning session can be a real paid deliverable, distinct from hands-on care. An offer should name sibling/partner involvement and the document parents receive.
Our decision: show concrete plan sections and explain remote versus hands-on help. Do not imitate this provider's scope or imply matching inclusions or qualifications.

2. The Mothergood — Postpartum Doula (Sunnyside, Queens)
https://www.themothergood.com/postpartum-doula
Current undated page. It separates a complimentary 30-minute discovery call from a paid 90-minute virtual planning session, publicly displayed at $125. Planning includes a personal postpartum plan, preparation and helpers. It also sells standalone three-hour visits and longer in-person packages, with remote support windows stated. The page welcomes first and subsequent babies and discusses siblings and household assistance.
Buyer expectation: free discovery is about fit; substantive individualized planning is paid. Packages need clear contact windows and meaningful differences.
Our decision: state the free 15-minute introduction's purpose, keep paid planning primary, and make the continuity package's two calls/two email rounds explicit from the shared catalogue. These adjacent prices do not justify Sleepy Llamas's price or prove willingness to pay. Do not import bodywork/herbal services or implied health outcomes from this offer.

3. The Mama Coach — Emily Musses, Newborn & Postpartum (Queens/NYC)
https://emily-musses.themamacoach.com/newborns/
Current page; no reliable publication date shown. Describes nurse-led newborn support, in-home or virtual. The standard package includes a 90-minute consultation, four weeks of weekly coaching calls, one month of email support and an eBook, displayed at $350–$500. A larger package and hourly support are also described. It provides a separate free 15-minute call.
Buyer expectation: time, contact frequency, reference material and format are concrete comparison criteria. Some competing newborn services are clinical or nurse-led.
Our decision: explain the actual consultation and included clarification email, rather than call it unlimited support. Do not use clinical assessment language or present Catalina as equivalent to a registered nurse, IBCLC or medical provider. The package is not a like-for-like price benchmark.

RECENT EDITORIAL / PROFESSIONAL MATERIAL

4. American Academy of Pediatrics, HealthyChildren — The 4th Trimester: Navigating Your First Few Months With a New Baby
https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/Pages/The-4th-Trimester-Navigating-Your-First-Few-Months-With-a-New-Baby-.aspx
By Natasha K. Sriraman; page updated July 28, 2026. Discusses newborn adjustment, feeding, soothing, parents' rest/support and clinical care when needed. Its framing includes partners and adoptive parents, not only a mother-baby pair. This is educational material, not evidence that parents will purchase a particular consultation.
Our decision: practical care coordination and parental support are substantive topics, and any health concerns belong with the family's clinical providers. Link the article as further reading, not as an endorsement or a replacement for care. Avoid reproducing medical instructions or statistics in sales copy.

5. DONA International — Postpartum Doula Standards of Practice
https://dona.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/DONA-Postpartum-Standards-of-Practice-2025.pdf
The document itself says last revised September 2025 (the January 2026 URL is not its revision date). Its scope covers nonmedical family adjustment, practical/emotional/informational support and referrals; it excludes diagnosing and treating. It applies to DONA's practitioners/candidates and does not verify Catalina's issuer or membership.
Our decision: describe a practical, nonmedical consultation, explicitly distinguish it from medical/mental-health/clinical feeding assessment and avoid fixed feeding or sleep promises. Do not borrow credential initials or claim this organization has approved Sleepy Llamas.

COPY AND IMPLEMENTATION DECISIONS

- Primary intent: newborn/postpartum consultation, practical first-days preparation, virtual/phone discussion and Queens/nearby NYC relevance. Keep geographic claims proportional: no invented borough coverage or nationwide professional authorization.
- Heading: a first-days plan with room for the whole family. Use distinct commercial content, not a generic parenting article or a list of keywords.
- Fit situations: preparing before birth; already home with practical questions; second/third baby and older siblings; helpers whose responsibilities need clarification.
- Session process: identify priorities; walk through practical care/support logistics alongside existing provider instructions; agree on next steps and questions requiring a specialist.
- Deliverable: priorities, adaptable daytime/nighttime care handoffs, who helps with what, sibling/visitor/household arrangements, and questions for the clinical team. No unverified guaranteed document delivery interval; confirm timing before booking.
- Follow-up: preserve the shared primary offer's one clarification email within seven days. The First Weeks Together secondary offer keeps its shared defined calls/email rounds and 14-day period. It is not a promise of unlimited messaging, same-day answers or round-the-clock access.
- Free fit call: 15 minutes to meet, discuss needs and choose service/timing. The personalized plan belongs to the paid session. CTAs must say request/ask while appointments are manually confirmed.
- Clinical boundary: no diagnosis, medication/feeding prescriptions, newborn sleep-training outcome guarantees, therapy or urgent response. Ask health providers about symptoms and feeding adequacy rather than attempting remote triage through the inquiry form.
- Premium proof: documented newborn twins and C-section-recovery-support experience, returning to a Brooklyn family for their third newborn, completed certification without invented issuer, readable sample plan, and specific scope/follow-up. More adjectives, borrowed testimonials or staged family photos do not establish value.
- Conversion validation: record which situation/offer prompted inquiries, whether parents understand the call/home-visit distinction, what was unclear about the deliverable, and whether paid clients found the plan usable. Do not mistake page traffic or forum discussion for willingness to pay.
