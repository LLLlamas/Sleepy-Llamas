EXECUTIVE FINDINGS
1. The $125 general family call looks credible within the observed nonclinical parent/nanny consulting examples. It is above one experienced RIE/nanny educator's video equivalent (~$104/hour), below a national agency founder's $200/hour, and below some specialist parent-coaching offers. These are selected comparisons, NOT a market median.
2. The $185 newborn/postpartum call is a deliberate premium: 48% above the general call and 48–85% above an experienced Brooklyn doula's $125 video/$100 phone rate. It can be tested, but current evidence does not establish demand at $185. A personalized plan and follow-up need to be clear, and credentials must be verified. A newborn topic alone does not automatically create 48% more willingness to pay.
3. The $395 three-hour home consultation is much easier to defend on observed one-off consulting comparisons than a simple comparison with $60–$85 hourly doula shifts suggests. BrooklynDoula's published formula produces $375 for three hours, while Sleepy includes an additional 20-minute call. The same $395 is nevertheless premium relative to recurring support hours; explain that this is a focused consultation with preparation, practice and written deliverables.
4. The $395 first-weeks package has a value-communication problem: $185 initial newborn call + 2 x $65 follow-ups = $315. The bundle costs $80 more (25.4%) for an updated plan and an additional email round. This is not necessarily wrong, but it is not a discount bundle. Show substantive extra work before calling it better value. Do not change the rate without owner decision.
5. The $425 three-hour family-specific caregiver session is credible as private household coaching plus a care guide and follow-up; the evidence for exact NYC demand is thinner. Do not compare it directly with the per-student tuition of professional, group or accredited courses.
6. Overnight package prices translate into roughly $56–$62/hour if every shift is ten hours, within several NYC providers' observed ranges and below selected $65–$85 examples. Contracted shift length is essential: a package dollar amount without hours cannot be benchmarked reliably.

METHOD AND LIMITATIONS
Primary sources were providers' own current public service/rate pages, accessed on the date above. Providers' credential descriptions are self-reported, not independently verified. Publication/update dates were often absent; “available on this date” is not assurance that the checkout price or availability is unchanged. No providers or clients were contacted. Prices do not reveal utilization, willingness to pay, conversion, profitability, quality or outcomes. Selection was purposeful for useful comparability, not representative sampling.

Contact-hour normalization = advertised price divided by specified live contact hours. It is not profit and may omit prep, notes, messaging, travel, acquisition and admin. Missing durations are explicitly unnormalized. For bundles with unspecified follow-up call lengths, do not invent an exact hourly comparison. No market median/percentile is claimed.

Different products must stay separate: practical doula consultation; recurring hands-on postpartum shifts; nanny/family consulting; licensed RN/IBCLC assessment; older-infant sleep coaching; family workshop; professional credential course; group classes; staffing/placement fees.

THE 48% NEWBORN PREMIUM
$185/$125 = 1.48. Premium is $60 for the same live duration. It is commercially plausible if newborn work requires more preparation, specific verified doula education, a more detailed plan and careful referral judgment. It is not justified merely because the family is anxious or the baby younger. Parents should understand what expertise and work they receive. General family calls already involve real expertise; do not demean those services or imply all older-child concerns are simple.
The observed close nonclinical NYC video benchmark is $125/hour, with another local provider at $100/hour plus two emails; national certified doula example is $75. Thus $185 should be described internally as premium-to-this-observed-subset, not “the normal NYC rate.” Licensed medical/lactation fees cannot validate a nonclinical premium.

THE FIRST-WEEKS PACKAGE ARITHMETIC
Current standalone newborn call $185 + 2 newborn follow-ups at $65 = $315. Draft bundle $395 adds $80/25.4%. Initial call already includes plan and one clarification email; bundle includes revised plan and two question rounds. Some email overlap exists. If $80 represents substantive extra asynchronous review and written work, show it concretely. If not, the package may appear to penalize commitment. A lower hourly gross after labor can still coexist with poor perceived value. Keep current rate untouched while deciding scope/price later. Do not label it a saving without honest comparable arithmetic.

WHY HOME VISIT CAN COST LESS PER CONTACT HOUR THAN A CALL
Newborn call $185/hour versus home $395/3h = $131.67/hour before the included follow-up; $118.50/hour including that call. A consultation call may be concentrated analysis with prep/written synthesis; a home visit mixes discussion, demonstration, practice and household setup. Different contact intensity and a three-hour commitment can rationalize different rates. Yet home visits add travel and block larger chunks of time, so their labor margins can be lower. Cap travel geography, define three priorities and clarify that home visit is focused consulting rather than three hours of unrestricted childcare/housework. Do not present the home visit as three repeated $185 calls.

WHAT TO LEARN BEFORE CHANGING PRICES
- Confirm which credentials are complete/current and display exact titles; no invented RN/IBCLC accreditation.
- Interview prospective parents about a recent real need, what they paid/considered, what deliverable would matter; do not ask only “would you pay $185?”
- Test paid demand with consistent scope, track inquiries/qualified buyers/paid conversion and reasons lost. Small samples are directional, not statistically decisive.
- Track preparation, notes, messages, follow-up, travel and unpaid fit-call time by service. Include payment fees, insurance and cancellations in contribution analysis.
- For bundle, ask clients to explain in their own words what extra value they see versus $315 a-la-carte. If they cannot, the issue may be product design rather than absolute price.
- Avoid huge introductory discounts or unlimited messaging; they obscure sustainable willingness to pay and workload.
- For training, separate the parent buyer's goal (household alignment) from caregiver buyer's goal (career credential). Never imply a three-hour family session confers professional certification.
- Reassess after initial paid engagements and recorded hours; current public competitor listings cannot establish what this particular business will sell.
